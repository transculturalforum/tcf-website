Community Q&A Board

Q

I've noticed that several cultures have a version of a "Festival of Lights." Is there a historical connection between Diwali in Hinduism and Hanukkah in Judaism?

Submitted by: Marcus T.
A

Transcultural Forum Admin: Great question, Marcus! While they are both beautifully centered around the symbolism of light triumphing over darkness, there is no direct historical or theological link between the two. Diwali has ancient roots in the Indian subcontinent, marking the triumphant return of Lord Rama (among other traditions depending on the region). Hanukkah commemorates a specific historical event in 2nd-century BCE Judea—the rededication of the Second Temple. The shared theme of "light" is a universal human response to the approaching winter months and the innate desire for hope in the dark!

Q

Can you explain the difference between 'Secular Humanism' and simply being 'Atheist'?

Submitted by: Sarah J.
A

Transcultural Forum Admin: Absolutely. "Atheism" is simply the lack of belief in a god or gods—it tells you what a person doesn't believe, but nothing about their morals or worldview. "Secular Humanism," on the other hand, is an active, positive philosophy. It states that since we do not rely on supernatural intervention, human beings have the profound responsibility to shape their own lives, create their own meaning, and act ethically based on empathy, reason, and a desire to reduce human suffering.
